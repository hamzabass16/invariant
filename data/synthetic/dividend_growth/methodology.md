# The Income Ledger (fictional)

How I run my dividend portfolio. One brokerage account, around fifteen
companies, no options, no leverage.

## 1. Objective

1.1 The portfolio exists to pay a rising income. The measure of success is
the dividends received in the last twelve months and their growth against the
twelve months before, not the account's price.

1.2 Income should grow at least 7% a year, from raises and reinvestment
together.

## 2. What I own

2.1 Every company pays a dividend it has raised for at least ten consecutive
years.

2.2 Payout ratio at or below 60% of earnings.

2.3 Dividend growth over the last five years of at least 6% a year.

2.4 Yield on cost is shown for every holding and for the whole.

## 3. Diversification

3.1 No holding above 12% of the portfolio. Hard limit.

3.2 No sector above 25% of the portfolio.

3.3 At least twelve companies at all times.

## 4. Cash

4.1 Cash between 3% and 10% of the portfolio: enough to buy a dip, never so
much that it drags.

## 5. What I watch

5.1 Dividend income by month over the last two years, income by holding, and
the projected annual income from today's yields.

5.2 The dividend yield of each holding, so I can see what is cheap and what
has run.

5.3 The account's growth of 100 since inception and its three-year return,
for context only; I do not act on price.

## 6. Habits

6.1 Every dividend is reinvested within the month it arrives.

6.2 I add 2,000 dollars a month, every month.
